Thief RT

Thief Gold and Thief II: The Metal Age, and their fan missions, with path-traced lighting, shadows, reflections and god rays, running natively on Apple silicon Macs. Built on a restoration of the Dark Engine and the RTGL1 path tracer. An OpenGL renderer is included too.

You need: a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later; M3 or later recommended for ray tracing, because of their hardware ray tracing), macOS 14 or newer, and your own copy of the games. The games are not included.

1 Get the games' files

Thief RT plays the original games' data. Put each game's folder anywhere on your Mac: a Games folder in your home folder, Downloads or Documents. The installer looks there, in Applications and in Steam's folder, and you can also point it at a folder yourself.

GameVersionThe folder with
Thief GoldSteam or GOG (Thief: The Dark Project works too)cam.cfg, THIEF.EXE, the missions (miss1.mis …)
Thief II: The Metal AgeSteam or GOGcam.cfg, thief2.exe, the missions

These releases are made for Windows. To get their files onto a Mac, copy the game's folder from a Windows PC where it is installed, or unpack the GOG installer with innoextract (brew install innoextract, then innoextract setup_….exe). Mods the game's folder already has (such as the Thief Gold HD Mod, through its cam_mod.ini) are used as they are.

2 Fan missions, and The Black Parade

Fan missions go in the game's own FMs folder, each unpacked into a folder of its own (Thief Gold's missions in Thief Gold's FMs, Thief II's in Thief II's). The launcher lists them.

The Black Parade, the Thief Gold campaign, is found by the installer: leave its download (the .zip or .7z, or its unpacked folder) in your Downloads folder, and the installer offers to put it in Thief Gold's FMs folder.

3 Optional: the HD Mods

The Thief Gold HD Mod replaces the game's textures, models and skies with hand-made HD ones, and Thief RT makes its materials from them. The installer sets it up for you, in the original missions and in fan missions (with the mod's fan-mission versions of its torches and water):

  • If your Thief Gold folder already has it (an HDMOD folder), it is used as it is.
  • Otherwise, download its installer (Thief1HDMod1.2.exe) into your Downloads. It is a Windows installer: to unpack it on a Mac, install innoextract first (in Terminal: brew install innoextract). The installer then finds it and offers to install it (about 3 GB). An HDMOD folder copied from a Windows install, or an archive holding one, works too.

The installer writes Thief Gold's cam_mod.ini to use it, keeping the old one as cam_mod.ini.before-thief-rt. To keep the HD Mod out of fan missions, replace its fm_mod_path line with mod_path_skip_fm.

The Thief II HD Mod (ModDB, Nexus Mods; version 1.0, by the same author) does the same for Thief II: HD textures, objects, skies and water. Download its full installer into your Downloads the same way (it needs innoextract too, and takes about 5 GB once installed); the installer, or the launcher's File → Find Games Again, finds it and offers to install it into Thief II's folder. An HDMOD folder copied from a Windows install of Thief II works too.

4 Install

  1. Open the disk image and double-click Install Thief RT.
  2. The first time, macOS says it can't check the app, because it isn't from the App Store or a registered developer. Click Done, then open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to "Install Thief RT". Confirm with your password.
    Or, in Terminal: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Volumes/Thief RT/Install Thief RT.app", then open it.
  3. The installer puts Thief RT in Applications and shows the games it found; choose which to use.
  4. macOS keeps an app that is not installed yet out of your Downloads, Documents and Desktop folders. If your games are there, the installer can't see them: then Thief RT looks for them itself the first time you open it. When macOS asks whether Thief RT may read those folders, allow it. (Later: File → Find Games Again.)

5 Play

Open Thief RT. Its window has these choices:

GameThief Gold or Thief II. A game in another folder: File → Choose a Game Folder.
PlayThe original campaign, or a fan mission from that game's FMs folder (The Black Parade first).
GraphicsRay traced (the path tracer) or OpenGL (the game's own look with sharper textures, and real-time lights and shadows from torches and lamps; fast on any Apple silicon Mac).
QualityFor ray tracing: Ultra, High (recommended), Medium or Low. They set the resolution, the upscaling (MetalFX), the bounce light, god rays and how many lights are traced at once. Dynamic resolution keeps 60 fps in all of them: on a slower Mac, a lower preset gives a sharper picture at the same frame rate.
OpenGL optionsWith OpenGL chosen: Resolution (100% is the screen's own; 75% or 67% is much faster on a 4K or 5K display, the menus and the HUD staying sharp), Real-time lights (how many of the nearest lamps and torches light and shadow the scene in real time; Off is the game's original lightmaps), Shadows from objects, Bump maps, and V-sync (Adaptive, On, or Off: the frame rate unlocked, with tearing possible).
  • In the game, Cmd+, (or F10) opens the ray tracing settings: the preset, resolution, frame rate, brightness and the effects. Everything finer and more technical is under Developer settings at the end of the list. Reset to defaults brings back High. With OpenGL, the same keys open its settings (the options above, and whether guards notice your shadow, and how far unlit corners are lifted from black).
  • Controls added to the game's own: hold Tab for the inventory wheel (weapons and items; move the mouse to pick, let go to take it), and Shift+Q / E lean as Q / E do. The middle mouse button blocks, as in the original. Items still cycle with [ and ]. Options → Controls changes any of them.
  • The first time you play each level, its surface materials are made and kept, so the level can look plain for a few seconds.
  • Dark areas look too bright, or crushed, on an external display? In the settings, raise Deep shadows (display) for an LCD whose blacks are grey.
  • For the smoothest play, close or minimise busy windows such as a browser playing video: they share the graphics chip with the game.
  • Show the frame rate (FPS) in the launcher turns the frame counter on or off, for either renderer; in the game, the settings (Cmd+, or F10) have it too. The launcher's Developer Settings… shows the logs and settings folders.

6 Where things are

Launcher/Applications/Thief RT.app (or ~/Applications)
SavesIn the game's own folder, as always (saves; a fan mission's in its folder in FMs)
Settings~/Library/Application Support/Dark Engine Restored (rt_settings.txt, gl_settings.txt)
Engine, materials, logs~/Library/Application Support/Thief RT (logs/thief.log if something goes wrong)

Updating: run the new version's installer. Saves, settings and the materials made so far are kept.
Uninstalling: delete Thief RT from Applications and the folder ~/Library/Application Support/Thief RT. Your game files are never changed (except the saves the game makes in them).
